Research

Five connected lines of enquiry

My work runs in both directions between physics questions and detector technology. Precise measurements of neutrino properties need better detectors; better detectors, in turn, open applications far beyond particle physics.

  1. Neutrino & Astroparticle Physics

    Neutrino mass ordering, matter effects and leptonic CP violation in atmospheric and long-baseline experiments, with simulation and machine-learning analysis.

  2. Solid-State Detector R&D

    Low Gain Avalanche Diodes, AC-LGADs for 4D tracking, and diamond sensors: design, TCAD simulation, characterisation and radiation hardness.

  3. Gaseous Detector R&D

    Resistive Plate Chambers and Multi-gap RPCs for large-area, fast-timing detection, and Time Projection Chambers.

  4. Beam & Plasma Diagnostics

    Ultra-fast, radiation-hard sensors and readout for synchrotrons, X-ray free-electron lasers, accelerators and plasma experiments.

  5. Medical Imaging & Muon Tomography

    Time-of-flight PET with MRPCs, detectors for hadron therapy, and imaging with cosmic-ray muons.

Biography

A path from neutrinos to sensors

Dr. Mohammad Nizam is an Assistant Professor of Physics at Xiamen University Malaysia, a Research Associate at the Santa Cruz Institute for Particle Physics (SCIPP), University of California Santa Cruz, and an Adjunct Assistant Professor at Xiamen University, China.

He received his PhD from Homi Bhabha National Institute (Bhabha Atomic Research Centre), Mumbai, for research carried out as a Research Scholar at the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR). His doctoral work combined hadron energy reconstruction for the magnetised Iron Calorimeter (ICAL) of the India-based Neutrino Observatory with the development of Multi-gap Resistive Plate Chambers for time-of-flight PET. He holds MSc and BSc degrees from Aligarh Muslim University.

After a period as Visiting Postdoctoral Fellow at TIFR, he joined SCIPP as a postdoctoral researcher, and he has also been a Research Affiliate at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. At SCIPP he worked on AC-coupled LGADs for 4D tracking, electronic-grade diamond sensors for X-ray and accelerator beam diagnostics, radiation damage, and ultra-fast readout electronics. He now leads a research group in Malaysia and contributes to the CERN DRD3 collaboration on solid-state detectors.
