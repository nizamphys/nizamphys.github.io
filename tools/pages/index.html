  • Assistant Professor of Physics, Xiamen University Malaysia

I study neutrinos, the most elusive particles in nature, with large-volume detectors and data from neutrino telescopes. I also develop detector technologies: fast silicon and diamond sensors for collider experiments and beam and plasma diagnostics, and gaseous detectors for large-area tracking, medical imaging and muon tomography.

Research

Five connected lines of enquiry

Explore the research

  1. Neutrino & Astroparticle Physics

    Mass ordering and CP violation with atmospheric neutrinos, and searches for new physics with astrophysical neutrinos in IceCube public data.

  2. Solid-State Detectors

    LGADs, AC-LGADs and diamond sensors for 4D tracking at colliders and for diagnostics: TCAD simulation, characterisation, radiation hardness.

  3. Gaseous Detectors

    Resistive Plate Chambers and Multi-gap RPCs for large-area, fast-timing detection, and Time Projection Chambers.

  4. Beam & Plasma Diagnostics

    Fast, radiation-hard sensors and readout for synchrotrons, X-ray free-electron lasers, accelerators and fusion plasmas.

  5. Medical Imaging & Muon Tomography

    Time-of-flight PET with MRPCs, hadron-therapy monitoring, and imaging with cosmic-ray muons.

Biography

From neutrinos to sensors

Short biography for talks and introductions.

Dr. Mohammad Nizam is an Assistant Professor of Physics at Xiamen University Malaysia, a Research Associate at the Santa Cruz Institute for Particle Physics (SCIPP), University of California Santa Cruz, and an Adjunct Assistant Professor at Xiamen University, China.

He received his PhD from Homi Bhabha National Institute (Bhabha Atomic Research Centre), Mumbai, for research carried out at the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR). His doctoral work combined hadron energy reconstruction for the magnetised Iron Calorimeter (ICAL) of the India-based Neutrino Observatory with the development of Multi-gap Resistive Plate Chambers for time-of-flight PET. He holds MSc and BSc degrees from Aligarh Muslim University.

After a period as Visiting Postdoctoral Fellow at TIFR, he was a postdoctoral fellow at SCIPP from 2020 to 2024 and, in parallel, a Research Affiliate at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, working on AC-coupled LGADs for 4D tracking, diamond sensors for X-ray and accelerator beam diagnostics, radiation damage and ultra-fast readout electronics. He now leads a neutrino physics and instrumentation group in Malaysia and contributes to the CERN DRD3 collaboration on solid-state detectors.
