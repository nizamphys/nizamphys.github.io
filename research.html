I

Neutrino & Astroparticle Physics

Neutrinos are the most abundant matter particles in the universe and the least understood. We still do not know the ordering of their masses, their absolute mass scale, or whether they violate CP symmetry, which may help explain why the universe contains more matter than antimatter.

At TIFR I developed hadron energy reconstruction for the magnetised Iron Calorimeter (ICAL) of the proposed India-based Neutrino Observatory using Geant4, and with colleagues showed that ICAL can distinguish matter-modified from vacuum oscillations of atmospheric neutrinos at 3σ in ten years. We also traced the disagreement between T2K and NOνA on the CP phase to their electron-neutrino appearance data.

My group now applies machine learning to atmospheric neutrinos: separating neutrinos from antineutrinos to improve CP-violation sensitivity in a 20-kton liquid-scintillator detector, studying its sensitivity to the mass ordering, rejecting atmospheric neutrino and muon backgrounds for the TRIDENT deep-sea neutrino telescope, and searching for new physics with ultra-high-energy astrophysical neutrinos.

Open questions

  • Neutrino mass ordering and absolute mass
  • Matter effects in oscillations
  • CP violation in the lepton sector
  • New physics with astrophysical neutrinos
  • Neutrinoless double beta decay; dark matter

Key papers

  • Matter vs vacuum oscillations, Nucl. Phys. B (2020)
  • T2K–NOνA tensions, Mod. Phys. Lett. A (2020)
  • Hadron energy estimation, Pramana (2020)
  • ML-assisted ν/ν̄ discrimination, arXiv (2026)
II

Solid-State Detector R&D

Future colliders and rare-event experiments need sensors that measure position and time together, to tens of picoseconds, while surviving intense radiation. Low Gain Avalanche Diodes (LGADs) achieve this with a thin internal gain layer.

At SCIPP I worked on AC-coupled LGADs, whose resistive readout shares the signal between neighbouring electrodes and gives fine spatial resolution with fewer channels. We studied signal sharing with lasers and test beams and long-distance signal propagation along strips, and I carried out TCAD simulations of their charge collection. This work supports 4D tracking for future facilities, including the high-granularity active target proposed for the PIONEER experiment.

I also study electronic-grade single-crystal CVD diamond, which is fast and radiation hard. In a 67.5 MeV proton beam at the Crocker Nuclear Laboratory, UC Davis, we followed a 40 µm diamond sensor in real time up to a fluence of about 4 × 1016 p/cm² and measured a damage constant of (3.3 ± 0.3) × 10−18 cm²/(p·µm). My group continues with TCAD studies of radiation damage in diamond, within the CERN DRD3 collaboration on solid-state detectors.

Technologies

  • LGADs and AC-LGADs (resistive silicon detectors)
  • Single-crystal CVD diamond sensors
  • Fast front-end electronics and ASICs
  • Radiation-damage studies

Key papers

  • Radiation damage in diamond, JINST (2024)
  • Long-distance signal propagation in AC-LGAD, NIM A (2024)
  • Charge sharing in AC-LGADs, NIM A (2023)
  • TCAD simulation of AC-LGADs, arXiv (2023)
III

Gaseous Detector R&D

Gaseous detectors remain the most economical way to instrument very large areas with good timing. Resistive Plate Chambers (RPCs) are the active layers of the ICAL design, and their multi-gap version (MRPC) reaches the time resolution needed for time-of-flight measurements.

At TIFR I built and characterised single-gap RPCs and six-gap glass MRPCs, and used stacks of RPCs to reconstruct cosmic-muon tracks. At Xiamen University Malaysia my students are now simulating, fabricating and testing RPCs and MRPCs, building local capability in gaseous detectors for imaging and muon tomography.

Technologies

  • Resistive Plate Chambers (RPCs)
  • Multi-gap RPCs (MRPCs)
  • Time Projection Chambers

Key papers

  • Six-gap MRPC for time-of-flight PET, JINST (2019)
  • Six-gap glass MRPCs, arXiv (2019)
IV

Beam & Plasma Diagnostics

Synchrotrons and X-ray free-electron lasers deliver intense, very short pulses, and users need to know the position and intensity of each pulse without blocking the beam. Thin diamond sensors can sit in the beam as pass-through monitors.

Within the Advanced Accelerator Diagnostics collaboration of UC campuses with LBNL, LANL and SLAC, I helped develop diamond-based diagnostics for synchrotron and next-generation XFEL beams, integrated multi-GHz detection systems, and fast readout such as the Fastpulse Precision Sampler ASIC. I also characterised LGADs with synchrotron X-rays and contributed to a submerged wireless imaging monitor for the Isotope Production Facility at Los Alamos National Laboratory.

The same fast, radiation-hard sensing suits plasma physics. My group is studying the feasibility of diamond-based diagnostics for fusion plasmas.

Applications

  • X-ray pass-through beam monitors
  • Multi-GHz accelerator diagnostics
  • Isotope-production beam monitoring
  • Fusion plasma diagnostics

Key papers

  • Diamond X-ray pass-through diagnostic, J. Synchrotron Rad. (2022)
  • Diamond diagnostics for XFELs, NIM A (2023)
  • X-ray detection with LGADs, JINST (2023)
V

Medical Imaging & Muon Tomography

Detectors built for particle physics often become tools for medicine and society. Positron Emission Tomography improves when each photon pair is timed precisely. My doctoral work showed that a time-of-flight PET system based on MRPCs is feasible and could offer large coverage at modest cost, and my group is now developing MRPCs for PET imaging.

I am also interested in fast-timing sensors for hadron-therapy monitoring, and in muon tomography, which uses naturally occurring cosmic-ray muons to image dense or inaccessible structures.

Directions

  • Time-of-flight PET with MRPCs
  • Hadron-therapy monitoring
  • Muon tomography

Key papers

  • Time-of-flight PET with six-gap MRPC, JINST (2019)
  • MRPC-based PET device, PoS ICHEP2018 (2019)
